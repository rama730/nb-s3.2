# Law-enforcement and government request procedure

1. Route every request to the named grievance/legal contact. Support personnel must not disclose user data directly.
2. Verify the requesting authority, official contact information, signature, jurisdiction, legal basis, exact identifiers, requested period, and service method.
3. Preserve only specifically relevant records when a valid preservation duty exists. Record scope, start, reviewer, expiry, and renewals.
4. Challenge or seek clarification for requests that are invalid, informal, overbroad, disproportionate, conflict with law, or cannot identify an account.
5. Search and disclose only data within scope using auditable, least-privilege procedures. A second authorised reviewer must approve production.
6. Notify the affected user before disclosure unless prohibited, unsafe, or an emergency exception lawfully applies. Record the basis for withholding or delaying notice.
7. Transfer records securely, log exactly what was disclosed, and retain the request, validation, approvals, response, and delivery confirmation under restricted access.
8. Emergency requests must identify the imminent risk, people at risk, information needed, and authority. Escalate immediately, disclose no more than necessary, and conduct retrospective review.

Qualified local counsel must review requests before production except where a documented, lawful emergency process applies.
