# Privacy and security incident response

1. Open an incident record with time, reporter, systems, suspected data, affected users, and incident lead. Do not copy sensitive payloads into ordinary chat.
2. Contain the incident: revoke tokens/sessions, isolate affected jobs or integrations, preserve logs, and stop further disclosure without destroying evidence.
3. Assess scope and risk: confidentiality, integrity, availability, data categories, number and location of people, likely harm, and whether a subprocessor is involved.
4. Engage the operator's security, privacy, legal, and grievance contacts. Notify insurers or contracted customers when applicable.
5. Determine regulator and user notification duties and deadlines for each relevant jurisdiction. Document the decision even if notification is not required.
6. Send accurate notices through verified channels. Describe what happened, likely consequences, mitigation, steps users can take, and a contact point; do not speculate.
7. Remediate, validate the fix, rotate affected credentials, restore safely, and monitor for recurrence.
8. Close with a root-cause review, corrective actions, retained evidence, notification copies, owners, and due dates. Restrict the incident record and apply the retention register.

Never promise a notification deadline without first applying the law and contracts relevant to the incident. The incident lead must obtain qualified legal advice for a reportable breach.
